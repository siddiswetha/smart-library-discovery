# The story — Smart Library Discovery
![Diya — example student avatar](../assets/avatar-diya.svg)

**This is an EXAMPLE fictional story.** It is not a real student, a real quote, or a real result. It shows, in plain language, the problem this project solves.

## Meet Diya

Diya is writing a psychology assignment and needs a statistics book. She does not remember the exact title or the author's spelling. She searches the library catalogue by topic, gets no useful result, and concludes that the library does not have the book.

Diya is about to buy the book online, even though she cannot really afford it. She is also thinking of citing a weaker online source instead, which would weaken her assignment.

## Before the product

The catalogue needs an exact title and correct spelling. Diya searches the way students actually search — by topic and by a half-remembered title — and fails. She loses out, and the library loses the evidence that its books are used.

## After the product

Diya types her topic and the part of the title she remembers. The system understands the query, searches only the library's own catalogue data, and shows the exact book in the top results, with its edition, shelf location, and live availability.

Close alternatives are shown separately, with a plain reason why each one is an alternative. Diya goes to the shelf, finds the book, and uses a real source for her assignment.

## What the AI PM learns from this

- Students search by topic and by partial memory, not by exact title.
- A failed search is a discovery failure, not proof the book is missing.
- Availability and edition must always be correct. A false "available" sends a student to an empty shelf.
- The product should use AI only where it adds value, and plain search where plain search is enough.
