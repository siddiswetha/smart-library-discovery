# 02 — Research Notes

## Purpose

Learn how students actually search, and what the catalogue data can and cannot show — before deciding what to build.

**Research plan: 5 students + 1 librarian.**

- **5 students**, each doing a real assignment, observed (with permission) doing real catalogue searches.
- **1 librarian**, interviewed about availability, editions, and shelf locations in the catalogue data.

> **Important:** No real interviews have been conducted yet for this project. Everything below is a template and EXAMPLE questions. Do not present any quote in this file as a real user quote. Fill in the tables only after real sessions have taken place.

## Part A — Student research (5 students)

### How to run each session

1. Ask the student to bring a real assignment, not a made-up task.
2. Ask them to search the catalogue as they normally would.
3. Watch and take notes. Do not correct their spelling or guide them to the "right" search.
4. Ask them to think aloud: what are they looking for, and what did they expect the results to show?
5. After the search, ask what they will do next if they cannot find the book.

### Template interview questions

These are EXAMPLE template questions. Use them as a starting point, not a script to read word for word.

1. What is your assignment about, in your own words?
2. What book or source are you trying to find?
3. Show me how you would search for it. What will you type first?
4. What did you expect to see in the results?
5. Did you find a book you could use? How do you know it is the right one?
6. If you did not find it, what will you do next — buy it, use another source, ask at the desk, or go without it?
7. How do you check whether a book is available, and which edition you need?
8. What part of searching the catalogue is most frustrating?

## Part B — Librarian interview (1 librarian)

### Purpose

Find out what the catalogue data can and cannot show — especially availability, edition, and location — before the product promises any of those things.

### Template interview questions (EXAMPLE)

1. What does the catalogue record for each book: title, author, edition, location, availability?
2. How is availability updated, and how fresh is it? Is it live, hourly, or daily?
3. What does "available" mean in the data — on the shelf, or only "not checked out"?
4. How are editions recorded? Can a student tell the 3rd edition from the 4th edition in the catalogue?
5. How are shelf locations recorded? Are they exact enough to walk to, or only a floor or section?
6. What is the most common question at the help desk about finding a book?
7. What goes wrong most often when a student cannot find a book?
8. What would you need to see before you trusted a new search tool enough to recommend it to students?
